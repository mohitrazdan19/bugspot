12 checkout bugs testers miss, and how to catch them

The checkout is where an app makes its money, and where small bugs cost the most. Here are twelve checkout bugs that slip through testing, grouped by where they hide.

A checkout screen looks simple: a few items, a total and a Pay button. But it brings together prices, offers, tax, delivery, stock, currency and a payment gateway, and each one can go wrong on its own. Most checkout bugs are not crashes. They are numbers that are slightly wrong, which is why they get past a quick look and why customers notice them first.

The bugs below come from our Bug library. Each one links to a fake app screen where you can try to find it yourself.

Part 1: the maths

The single most useful habit in checkout testing is to add the numbers up yourself, on every screen, every time. A calculator is a testing tool.

1 The total does not add up

Items come to ₹1,125, delivery is ₹40, and the total says ₹1,065 instead of ₹1,165. The page looks fine and nobody reads the numbers, except the customer and, later, the finance team. Totals go wrong when one line is left out, counted twice or rounded differently from the rest.

P1 · Critical · IntermediateSpot this bug →

2 The discount uses the wrong base

A 15% offer on ₹1,200 should save ₹180, so the customer pays ₹1,020. If the screen says ₹1,080, the discount was probably worked out on a different amount (such as the price after another offer) or rounded at the wrong step. Work out every percentage by hand.

Real example“You pay” does not match the 15% discount
P2 · Major · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

3 “You saved” goes negative

Savings lines are often calculated separately from the total, so they drift. A promo worth ₹50 shown as “You saved ₹-120” tells the customer the offer cost them money. Check that the saving matches the difference between the original and the final price, and that its sign is right.

Real example“You saved” shows ₹-120 instead of ₹50
P2 · Major · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

4 Tax is calculated wrongly

GST at 18% on ₹1,500 is ₹270. A screen that shows ₹207 has swapped two digits, and every invoice is now wrong. Tax bugs are always serious, because they create legal and accounting problems as well as unhappy customers. Test tax on at least two different amounts, and on amounts with paise, where rounding matters.

Real exampleGST at 18% on ₹1,500 is shown as ₹207 instead of ₹270

Part 2: across the journey

Many checkout bugs only appear when you compare one step with the next: cart, checkout, payment, confirmation and invoice should all tell the same story.

5 The price changes between cart and checkout

The cart shows ₹799, the checkout shows and charges ₹899. This usually comes from a cached price or an offer that expired between two screens. Customers see it as a bait and switch. Note the price of each item on the product page, in the cart, at checkout and on the invoice, and compare them.

Real exampleCheckout charges ₹899 for an item the cart shows at ₹799
P2 · Major · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

6 The wrong currency appears on one line

A rupee order with “Delivery $49” on one line looks careless at best and suspicious at worst, especially after a localisation change. Check every currency symbol, every thousands separator and every decimal on the summary, not only the total.

Real exampleDelivery fee shown in dollars on a rupee order
P3 · Minor · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

7 Quantities below one are accepted

Tap minus on a quantity of 1 and the cart shows −1 with a negative price. The same bug often exists in the cart API, where the screen’s limits do not apply at all. Try 0, −1 and a very large quantity, both in the app and by calling the API directly.

Real exampleCart accepts quantity -1 and lowers the total
P2 · Major · IntermediateSpot this bug →

Part 3: the screen itself

Checkout screens are often used on small phones, on slow networks and in a hurry. Layout bugs that would be minor elsewhere can stop a payment here.

8 The keyboard hides the Pay button

On a small phone, tapping the last field opens the keyboard, which covers the Pay button, and the page will not scroll. The customer cannot finish the order without closing the keyboard, and some will simply give up. Always test forms with the keyboard open, on a small screen, in both orientations.

Real exampleOn-screen keyboard hides the Pay button
P2 · Major · IntermediateSpot this bug →

9 A button covers the price

On a narrow screen, or with a larger text size, the “Add to cart” button can be drawn on top of the price. Test with the phone’s text size turned up, with long product names and in other languages, which are often longer than English.

Real exampleThe “Add to cart” button covers the price
P3 · Minor · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

10 The total is hard to read

Light grey text on a white background might match the design, but the most important number on the page should be the easiest to read. Normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1. Free contrast checkers will tell you in seconds.

Part 4: the payment

The last step is where the most expensive bugs live, because real money has already moved.

11 “Payment failed”, but the money was taken

The screen says the payment failed, and right below it the order is confirmed and charged. The customer will either pay again or call support, and both cost money. This usually happens when the app times out before the payment gateway answers. Test slow networks, timeouts and what the app does when the answer arrives late.

Real exampleScreen says “Payment failed” while the order is confirmed and charged
P1 · Critical · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

12 A retry charges the customer twice

Payment systems use an idempotency key so that the same request, sent twice, is only processed once. If the server ignores the key, a double tap, a network retry or a refresh can charge the customer twice. Test the Pay button with a double tap, a refresh during payment and a poor connection, and check the payments list afterwards.

Real exampleRetry with the same idempotency key charges the customer twice
P1 · Critical · ExperiencedSpot this bug →

Your checkout test checklist

  • Items + delivery + tax − discounts = total, added up by hand
  • Every percentage worked out on the right base amount
  • Savings are positive and match the real difference
  • Tax is right on at least two amounts, including paise
  • The same price on the product page, cart, checkout and invoice
  • One currency and one number format on every line
  • Quantity 0, −1 and very large values are blocked, in the app and the API
  • Pay button reachable with the keyboard open on a small phone
  • Readable with a larger text size and in other languages
  • One clear result after payment, even on a slow network
  • Double tap and refresh during payment charge only once

Print this list, or keep it open next to the app the next time you test a checkout. Most of these checks take less than a minute each, and between them they cover the bugs that cost real money.

Frequently asked questions

What should I test first on a checkout page?

The maths. Add up the items, delivery, tax and discounts by hand and compare the result with the total on screen. Money bugs are the most serious and the easiest to miss.

How do I test a payment without being charged?

Use the test or sandbox mode of the payment gateway, with its test card numbers. Never test payments with real cards on production unless your team has a process for it.

What is an idempotency key?

A unique value sent with a payment request so the server can tell when the same request arrives twice, for example after a retry, and process it only once.

Why test checkout on a small phone?

Many customers pay on a phone. The keyboard, small screens and large text sizes cause layout bugs, such as a hidden Pay button, that never appear on a laptop.
